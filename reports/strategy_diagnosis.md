# Strategy diagnosis — why the base strategy loses money

Generated by `scripts/diagnose_strategy.py` (raw numbers in `strategy_diagnosis.json`).
No parameter was tuned. Costs come from the engine defaults: 0.10% fee + 0.02% slippage per side, so a round trip costs **24 bps**. Starting equity is 10,000. Sizing uses a fractional step (BTC 0.00001, ETH 0.0001). The exit is the engine default. Data covers BTCUSDT/ETHUSDT 15m and 1h, about 2 years of Binance spot (70k bars at 15m, 17.5k at 1h).
This is not investment advice.

## 2. Diagnosis, ranked by certainty

### (a) The signal has no edge — **highest certainty**
- **Exit-free test.** I measured the signed return from the next bar's open to the close h bars later (h = 1, 4, 12, 24), after each of the 3,645 signals. In 15 of 16 cells the mean is between −19 and +2.4 bps with |t| < 1.5. The one significant cell, BTC 1h h=4 (−13 bps, t = −2.9), points the *wrong* way. With 16 tests, one hit is consistent with chance. None of these numbers comes close to the 24 bps round-trip cost.
- **Realized gross PnL vs random entries.** Over 30 runs with the same bars, same trade count and same exits, but random direction, the production signal ranks at the 60th / 7th / 63rd / 0th percentile. It is no better than a coin flip.
- **Gross win rate.** It is 34–39%. The breakeven for a 1.5R target is 40%, which again fits zero edge.

### (b) Costs are large relative to the move — **certain, but this is the amplifier, not the root cause**
- 24 bps round trip against an average signal move of about 0 bps. On 15m, a 2-ATR stop is small, so costs reach 0.33–0.53R per trade. That is why the 15m sets lose about 90%.
- Even with costs at zero, gross R is ≤ 0 on 3 of 4 datasets. Cutting costs (a maker rebate or a lower tier) would only slow the losses down. It would not create a profit.

### (c) Sizing and exits — **real defects that distort the *measurement*, but they do not cause the loss**
1. **Sizing bug (confirmed and fixed).** `BacktestEngine` rounded quantity down to whole units. At 10k equity, BTC (~60k) always rounds to 0, so there were **0 trades**. ETH lost precision but still traded (852 trades with whole units vs 992 with fractional). Every earlier BTC backtest at the default equity was therefore empty or distorted.
   - Fix: `BacktestEngine(quantity_step=...)`. The default stays at 1.0 for backward compatibility. The research scripts should pass the instrument's lot step.
   - Risk per trade stays fixed at 1%, so this does not change R results.
2. **Exit naming and model mismatch (reported, not fixed).**
   - The default `exit_model="fixed_3r"` is really SL 2 ATR / TP 3 ATR, which is **1.5R, not 3R**. It even has a smaller target than `fixed_2r` (TP 4 ATR = 2R).
   - The live `Brain` uses `ExecutionModel` (by regime: SL 1.5/TP 3 ATR when trending, 1/2 ATR when ranging). The backtest uses a different exit, so it does not measure the live configuration exactly.
   - However, the exit-free test shows no edge at any horizon from 1 to 24 bars, so no exit choice can turn this signal profitable. I did not change the exit, to avoid tuning the backtest to look better.

**Overall:** the strategy loses because the signal (an MA 20/50 cross gated by RSI/regime) has no predictive power on this data. Costs then turn that roughly-zero gross result into a steady loss. Sizing distorted the measurement but is not the reason for the loss.

## 3. What this data cannot answer
- **No MT5 data.** The live bot trades MT5 symbols, and this repo has **no MT5 history at all** (no EURUSD M1/M15). Crypto results do not transfer to FX or gold. What's needed: MT5 history for the symbols the bot actually trades (`scripts/fetch_mt5_history.py` exists), with real per-bar spread, commission and contract size, run through `MT5CostModel`.
- **Only about 2 years of data.** That covers a limited set of regimes, so a weak edge could still be missed. It is enough to rule out an edge large enough to cover 24 bps.
- **Exits not tested as live.** The backtest does not use the live `ExecutionModel` exits. To test the live configuration exactly, the backtest needs an exit model that calls `ExecutionModel`. That is plumbing, not tuning.
